TabSonar — Privacy Policy

Last updated: July 2026

Short version: TabSonar collects nothing and sends nothing anywhere. It's a Chrome extension that runs entirely on your device.

What TabSonar does

TabSonar reads your open tabs (title, URL, favicon, audio state, last-used time) so it can show you a searchable list, detect duplicates, and free up memory. All of this happens locally, inside your browser.

What it stores, and where

  • Nothing leaves your device. TabSonar has no server, no analytics, and no network calls of its own.
  • Your language preference and a small "always keep this site" list are saved using Chrome's built-in chrome.storage API — local to your browser (the "keep" list syncs across your own signed-in Chrome profiles via Google's sync, the same as your bookmarks would).
  • A lifetime counter of auto-closed duplicates is stored locally the same way.
  • Tab favicons are loaded directly from the site that owns them (e.g. notion.so loads its own icon) — the same request your browser already makes when you open that site normally. TabSonar doesn't proxy, log, or see this request.

Permissions, and why

  • tabs — to list, search, focus, close, mute, and discard tabs.
  • storage — to remember your language and "keep" preferences.
  • system.memory — to show free RAM in the header.
  • alarms — to run a periodic background check for duplicate tabs.
  • sessions — to restore a tab you closed by mistake ("Undo") and to show recently-closed tabs.
  • tabGroups — to show an accurate tab-group count.

Third parties

None. No advertising SDKs, no analytics, no data brokers.

Changes

If this ever changes (it's not planned to), this page will be updated and the extension's version history will reflect it.
